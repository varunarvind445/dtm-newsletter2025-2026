From Living Room to “Lights, Camera, Horror!”: What My First Short Film Taught Me About Toastmasters

If someone had told me that my living room would turn into a movie set and my phone would become a professional camera, I would have laughed. Yet that’s exactly how my first short film—a horror story—began, with my two sons as the actors and my friend Supraja as my co-director. Our equipment? One mobile phone. Our crew? Two determined mothers and two surprisingly convincing child actors. We filmed entirely at home, turning corners into eerie “sets,” experimenting with shadows, and capturing suspenseful angles. My sons delivered performances that were at once innocent and unsettling—a perfect fit for a horror film. Every take brought unexpected surprises, from impromptu reactions to perfect eerie pauses. While filming was chaotic and fun, the real challenge started when we sat down to edit. Both of us stared at the editing software like it was an alien spaceship. We had no prior experience, but we dived into tutorials, tried every tool, and celebrated each tiny breakthrough. Then came the music. Once we added it, the film finally became what we intended: downright chilling. The score turned ordinary moments into tense, breath-held beats; my sons’ quiet looks felt ominous, and ordinary rooms became claustrophobic sets. Supraja and I listened with goosebumps, then went back to fine-tune the audio—balancing silence and sound so the suspense landed just right—until the film truly came alive as a horror story. Looking back, this journey reminded me so much of Toastmasters. Every new speech, evaluation, or role comes with a mix of excitement and uncertainty. You don’t always know how it will turn out, but you step forward anyway. You learn, adjust, improve, and eventually gain confidence—just as I did editing that horror film, frame by frame. Making my short film taught me that creativity grows when we dare to experiment, and confidence grows when we take the first step even without all the skills. Toastmasters teaches us the same lesson: show up, try, learn, repeat. Whether on stage or behind a camera, growth begins the moment we say, “Let me try.”

Archana Rajesh Kumar
Vice President Education
